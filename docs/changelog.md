# Lionheart Fixt - the changelog

Every release, newest first, one line each. Releases are **cumulative**: the latest zip contains all
of them, so there is never a reason to install more than one.

This is the release *map* -- the same rows the README carried until it got too long. A few small
patch releases share a row with the version they repair, and a few (0.2.1, 0.9.2 through 0.9.4) have
no row of their own; every tagged release, without exception, has a full section in
[`releases.md`](releases.md).

This is the short form. Each release is also written up in full -- the survey that found the work,
the reads behind every decision, what was left alone and why -- in
[`releases.md`](releases.md), and the cases a release had to pass are in [`qa.md`](qa.md).

The three most recent releases are also listed in the [README](../README.md); this file is the whole
history.

| **0.77.2** | Pestilence | **Left out of 0.77.0 and 0.77.1 twice over, on a reading given in both: disease is resisted by 168 of 422 races at a median of 100, so the type was the problem and no ceiling would fix it. Measured by spawn rather than by race, the wrong half of that was the half that mattered.** Disease is **binary**: **63%** of what the game spawns takes the full hit, **37%** takes none, and **0%** is partial, with 99.8% of the damage landing on any non-immune target. That median of 100 was never describing heavy partial resistance, it was describing immunity, and a graded number got read into a switch. The immune 5,783 spawns are coherent rather than scattered -- **Undead 4,334**, English Enemies 629, Animals 563, Sewers 248 -- so Pestilence is dead through the Crypt and act 7 and at **full strength everywhere else**. That makes it **conditional, like `Hunting` and `Exorcism`, not weak**, and blending it into a bestiary-wide average described no enemy that exists. It takes its place on the coverage ladder 0.77.0 built -- Goblin Slaying 3% at raw 25, Hunting 11% at 21, Exorcism 25% at 18, **Pestilence 63% at 15.5**, the ungated four at 100% and 11.0 to 13.5 -- which is now monotonic for the first time. Value band **10000 to 7000**, a ceiling no melee weapon in any save reaches so its stated 14 was unreachable as well as low; amount **14 to 26**; min term output **3 to 5**, which is not a round number picked by eye but the exact value `o1 = 1 + (A-2)/6` that preserves vanilla's slope across the widened input range, so the **median weapon is identical to vanilla to the digit** while the dearest goes 7.50 to **15.50**. Verified at 904 price points, worst delta +0.0000. `Carnage` and `Ripping` confirmed byte-identical to vanilla. **Flagged and deliberately not done here:** `Venom` and `Snakebiting` both deal poison, which is 40% immune and 2% partial -- the same shape as disease -- so both are conditional weapons priced as weak ones, and that is a change of its own |

The arithmetic was right and the prices were imaginary.** Read out of the baked instances in **71 saves**: 4,525 generated items carrying an addition, **908 of them melee weapons**, with item Value running min 2, **median 255**, 90th percentile 1,986, **dearest melee weapon 7,790**, and the dearest item of any kind 12,995. Each `min` term's **output** ceiling rises with its input, because moving the input alone widens the scale's domain and **lowers** the floor by about 1.3% -- a dip 0.77.0 shipped on three bands and this release also repairs. Verified at **904 price points** against the pristine archive: not one of the ten additions in the family is worse than vanilla anywhere. `Carnage`, `Pestilence` and `Ripping` are untouched by measurement as well as by intent |
