# Two lines for Sahar

For review. Nothing is in the tree. The fix in 0.71.2 made the peaceful route *work*; these two
lines are about making it *make sense*, which is a separate question and your call.

Both are additions to existing node text. No new nodes, no new replies, no structural change.

## Her register, so you can hold me to it

Measured off her nine existing lines:

| | |
|---|---|
| sentence shape | short declaratives, clipped, often a fragment first (*"You walked in. Good."*) |

## Line 1 -- why the player would agree

**The gap.** *"Then send me north"* is offered straight off `1 delivered` with no question asked, so
the likeliest path through the route is the one where the player agrees to walk into the Master's
hands having been told nothing about why north matters. She only explains it if the player happens to
ask about the Crown first, on `20 crown`: *"Over the mountains by now. You may follow it."*

One clause fixes it wherever the player enters, and it costs her nothing in character: she is not
persuading, she is stating a fact she finds mildly useful.

### node id: 1 delivered

**As it ships** (44 words):

> You walked in. Good. It saves rope. He wants you whole, and north, and I have no wagons left and no
> sisters I can spare to walk you over the mountains. I have been sitting on this step deciding what
> to do about that.

**Proposed** (60 words), one sentence added at the end:

> You walked in. Good. It saves rope. He wants you whole, and north, and I have no wagons left and no
> sisters I can spare to walk you over the mountains. I have been sitting on this step deciding what
> to do about that. **The Crown you came for is already over the mountains, if that helps you decide
> anything.**

**Why this and not something warmer.** It does three things at once: tells the player their own
objective went north, hands them the reason to accept, and does it as a shrug rather than an offer.
*"If that helps you decide anything"* is her, not me: it treats the player's decision as a formality,
which is exactly how she treats everything else about them. It also sets up her next line, since
*"A parcel that carries itself"* lands harder when the parcel has its own reason to walk.

## Line 2 -- why there is no offer for a player who fought in

**The gap.** `1 too late` is the node for a player who cut their way to the sanctum, and it carries
none of her motive. No wagons, no sisters, no problem she is sitting there solving. So that player
sees no trace that an offer ever existed, and the route reads as arbitrary from the only angle most
players will see it from.

**This does not open the route to them.** *"Then send me north"* stays gated on `delivered`. What it
does is make the absence of the offer a **consequence** rather than an omission: the player spent the
very things that would have made a peaceful handling possible.

### node id: 1 too late

**As it ships** (46 words):

> *&lt;She does not rise from the reliquary's step. Two of the creatures coil at her feet; a third,
> older, stands behind her with its hands folded.&gt;* You are late, Lionheart. The Master has what he
> came for, and I have what I was left to do.

**Proposed** (65 words), one sentence added at the end:

> *&lt;She does not rise from the reliquary's step. Two of the creatures coil at her feet; a third,
> older, stands behind her with its hands folded.&gt;* You are late, Lionheart. The Master has what he
> came for, and I have what I was left to do. **I had wagons for this once, and sisters I could
> spare. You have spent both on your way in.**

**Why this one is worth more than it looks.** It reuses her own two nouns from `1 delivered` --
wagons and sisters -- so the two openings read as the same woman with the same problem, differently
caused. And it turns a design limitation into something the fiction owns: the reason this player gets
no deal is that they killed the means of it. A player who hears that and reloads has learned a real
thing about the route, which is better than a player who never learns the route was there.

It is also the honest answer to the report that the path "makes zero sense". It did not, from that
angle, because the game never said why.

## What I am not proposing

- **No change to the player's reply.** *"Then send me north. Your word for my life, and I carry
  whatever you want carried."* already reads as a trade, and with Line 1 in place the player knows
  what they are trading for. Adding *"I am going that way regardless"* would say out loud what Line 1
  has just implied, and she is not a character who needs things said twice.
- **No change to `40 courier`.** Its text is fine now that it is no longer a trap.
- **No new stage direction.** She has two pieces in nine lines and both earn their place.

## If you want it shorter

Line 2 is the one that can go without breaking anything, and Line 1 is the one that fixes the actual
complaint. If only one ships, ship Line 1.
